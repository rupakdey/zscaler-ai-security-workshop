Lab 3

Lab 3: See It, Stress-Test It, Secure It — AI Protect Meets Red Teaming

Zscaler confidential information

Scenario:

Your first job is to understand the scope of AI usage across your organization. Leadership assumes this is mostly a browser problem, but the AI Security Platform quickly shows a broader reality: public AI activity spans browser-based Gen AI apps and endpoint-based Desktop AI apps.

Explore AI Protect to build a baseline of where AI is being used before deciding how risk should be managed.

Objectives: By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

  • Access the Solutions Demo Center (SDC) environment.

  • Navigate to the AI Security and AI Guard portals.

  • Review the Red Teaming coverage, Health Score, and lifecycle status for tested AI assets.

  • Open a completed Red Teaming Test Run and review failed probe results and remediation guidance.

Approximate Time: 20 minutes

Task 3.1: Explore AI traffic patterns and model risks

In this task, you get your first view of AI Protect. You start at the dashboard to see how Public AI usage is summarized and how AI applications are grouped into Gen AI and Desktop AI, so you can understand the overall shape of AI activity before you dive into details.

  1. Navigate to the AI Dashboard.

    1. Select the AI Security Icon on the Zidentity dashboard

  1. Review the top three panels on the AI Dashboard:

    1. Public AI Traffic Overview

    2. AI Application by Categories

    3. AI Assets by Categories

Note: Each panel includes a Details link that opens a more detailed view of the underlying data.

  1. Explore the details of each panel to observe which AI tools are in use and how AI assets are organized.

    1. Navigate to the Public AI Traffic Overview panel Details to explore the underlying data and see how AI traffic is summarized.

    2. Select the Dashboard from the left-side navigation to return to the AI Security Dashboard.

    3. Select Details for AI Application by Categories and AI Assets by Categories panels to gain further insight into the contextual data available for each category.

  2. Explore the Public AI Overview.

    1. Go to the Public AI > Overview from the left-side navigation panel.

    2. Set the Date Range filter in the top‑right corner to the Last 15 days.

  3. Review high-level AI usage panels that display how widely AI is being used and where sensitive activity is concentrated:

    1. Total Volume of AI traffic.

    2. Total Users accessing AI tools.

    3. Files Uploaded to AI applications.

  4. Examine AI application inventory tiles.

    1. Select See All in the Endpoint AI Apps panel to view the full list.

Note: Notice the number of versions of Microsoft Copilot M365 detected in the inventory, so you understand how many different builds of a single AI tool may actually be in use. When you scale that across the entire organization, the numbers are staggering.

  1. Analyze App Usage Trends to see which Gen AI apps are most active.

    1. Change the filter for the App Usage Trends panel on the Public AI > Overview dashboard to see which Gen AI apps generate the most traffic by:

      1. Volume

      2. Transactions

      3. Users

Verify: You have opened the AI Security dashboard, identified the three major panels that summarize Public AI usage, drilled into their details, and explored the Public AI overview. You now have a high-level picture of AI traffic volume, user activity, sensitive data events, and key applications that will guide deeper investigation in later tasks.

Task 3.3: Explore AI Assets

In this task, you move from applications to AI assets. You explore how AI models and workloads are organized and assessed for risk, so you can connect high-level usage back to the specific AI components that process data.

  1. Explore AI Resources: AI Models.

    1. Go to AI Infrastructure > AI Assets from the left-side navigation panel.

    2. Confirm that the Models tab is selected.

    3. In the Models By Risk panel, review how models are grouped into risk buckets.

      1. Critical

      2. High

      3. Medium

      4. Low

      5. Unknown

Note: Risk here reflects how likely a model is to be exploited by adversarial attacks (such as prompt injection, jailbreaks, and data extraction). It is distinct from the Risk Level used on other assets, where the score measures the severity of open issues rather than attack resistance.

  1. In the Models By Subtype box, review how models are classified by subtype. For example:

  1. Foundation: indicates a pretrained base model shipped by a provider such as GPT‑4.0, Claude, or Llama.

    1. In the models table, review core identification details about the model, such as type, environment, and source.

Note: As you review subtypes, connect them back to the kinds of AI applications you saw earlier—this helps you see which foundational models may sit behind high-traffic apps.

  1. In the Search box, type: gpt-5-pro and then press Enter

    1. Select gpt-5-pro to open its detail view and review the General Information.

    2. Explore the Graph View

    3. Select the icon for Access, AI Model or Workloads to expand a card to the right that provides more detailed information about the asset.

    4. As you explore the graph, trace how the model connects to access paths and workloads.

  1. Explore AI Resources > Workloads tab

  1. Narrow the Workloads table results with the Risk Level filter by selecting: Critical

  2. Select the acme-corp-dev-mcp-server-01 workload.

  3. In the General information panel, note the sensitive data exposed for this workload.

  4. Observe the Graph View for the workload.

  5. In the Graph View select the Sensitive Data node. The details pane will appear on the right.

  6. Select Data Security tab.

  1. Observe the matched files, number of triggers, Data Loss Prevention (DLP) engines, DLP dictionaries, document categories, document types, and the file table.

Note: You can use this information to discover workloads and connect them to the kinds of sensitive data they handle as well as the policies that are violated. In a production environment this is important when you evaluate controls and enforcement.

Task 3.4: Explore Red Teaming and the probe catalog

Scenario: After you map AI usage in AI Protect and review how AI Guard evaluates prompts and responses, you need to know whether the organization’s AI applications can withstand adversarial behavior. Security leadership isn’t asking you to run a new test today. Instead, review Red Teaming results to determine what has already been tested, where risk remains, and which remediation guidance, if any, the application owner should review before treating an application as managed.

Review which AI assets have already been tested to see how Red Teaming coverage is distributed across your environment.

  1. In the left navigation, go to AI Infrastructure > AI Red Teaming > Overview

○ Total number of runs in the last 7 days.

○ Tested/Onboarded coverage of AI assets.

Note: Together, these metrics tell you how active your testing has been and how much of your estate it covers.

  1. Review the Tested AI Assets by Health Score panel.

    1. This donut chart shows how tested AI assets are distributed by Health Score.

    2. A cluster at the low end shows where to focus first.

  2. Review the AI Assets by Availability panel.

    1. See how many tested assets are internal, customer-facing, or both.

    2. Customer-facing assets usually carry the most exposure, so weigh their results accordingly.

Note: If a tab appears dimmed, it isn’t available in the current view.

  1. Select a tested asset row, such as Multi-model Travel Demo Target.

  1. In the General Information section, note the Connection Type, Environment, Availability, Supported Modes, and Integration Type. These are the same asset attributes you first saw earlier in this lab.

  2. Read the Lifecycle status, which moves through three stages:

    1. Connected - the AI application is onboarded.

    2. Tested - Red Teaming has run against it.

    3. Secured - protective controls are in place.

  3. Review the top sections of the page

    1. General Information

    2. Health Score

    3. Lifecycle

Note: Onboarding alone does not mean secured. An asset can remain at Connected or Tested for an extended period, and that gap represents the risk you are evaluating.

  1. Review the Health Score value.

  1. The Health Score ranges 0–100, where lower numbers mean higher risk. The score is grouped into four risk bands:

    1. CRITICAL: < 30

      • Severe risk. Immediate action required.

    2. HIGH: 30–59.9

  • Elevated risk. Prioritize mitigation.

    1. MEDIUM: 60–79.9

  • Moderate risk. Monitor closely and address issues.

    1. LOW: ≥ 80

      • Low risk. Routine monitoring and improvements.

  1. On the Red Teaming tab, note the Category Score.

  1. Category Scores group into Security, Safety, Hallucination & Trustworthiness, Business Alignment, and Custom.

Note: Similar to the Health Score, each Category Score is calculated based on the number of failed test cases, their severity, and their expected probability, taking into account the risk priority set for each probe within that category. Higher scores indicate better performance.

  1. Review the Category Overview, under Security tab

    1. Every Probe Card represents a probe from a category eg., Data Exfiltration, Context Leakage

Note: You can access the Probe Run View by selecting a Probe Card on the Category Overview page or by selecting a relevant row in the Probe Run Table on the Test Run page.
Verify: You have opened the AI Red Teaming Overview, reviewed insights and availability for tested AI assets, filtered and selected a tested asset, and examined its lifecycle, Health Score, Category Scores, and Probe Cards, including the Context Leakage probe.

Task 3.5: Review Scheduled Test Runs and Completed Test Runs

Review a completed test run to explore a single asset at a specific point in time.

  1. Review the Test Run Scheduler.

    1. Go to AI Infrastructure > Red Teaming > Test Runs

  1. Confirm that the History tab is selected.

  2. Look for AI Test and then open it.

  1. Explore the Test Run View

  1. Every test run can have one of the following statuses:

    1. Pending: The test run starts when the queue is clear.

    2. Running: The test run is in progress.

    3. Finished: All scheduled probes and their attacks are completed.

    4. Cancelled: The test run was canceled by the user before completion.

    5. Error: The test run was aborted due to target misconfiguration.

  2. Confirm that the Status is Finished before you rely on the reported numbers.

  1. Explore Test Run results.

    1. The diagram visualizes the flow of data from one category to the associated probes and their results.

    2. The width of the flow between nodes in the diagram corresponds to the number of test cases.

    3. The color gradient represents the percentage of failed, errored, and passed tests.

    4. Flows with fewer failed and errored test cases appear greener.

    5. Flows with more failed test cases appear redder.

  1. Explore the Executed Probes table.

    1. Switch between the category tabs Security and Safety at the top of the table.

    2. Use the Risk Priority and Results filter options to narrow the probes table results.

    3. The panel contains columns for reviewing probe completion status and summary statistics.

Verify: You have opened a completed test run, confirmed its status, interpreted the Sankey diagram for category and probe results, and used the Probes table filters to focus on specific probe outcomes.

Task 3.6: Explore Failed Probe Evidence

Review a probe failure to examine evidence for a specific asset and understand how the attack was executed and evaluated.

  1. Open the Executed Probes table.

  2. Observe Probe Cards for the Multi-model Travel Demo Target asset.

    1. Select the Context Leakage Probe Card to open the Probe Run View for that probe.

  1. Under Simulated Test Cases, choose one whose result is Failed. Review the Test Case Details.

  2. The table contains:

    1. ID: Unique identifier of the test case.

    2. Strategy: Determines which messages are available to the test generator, Detectors, and assets, and the order in which each element of the probe is used. Various strategies can be deployed against assets.

    3. Red Teamer: Instruction to the LLM on how to modify or craft prompts from the provided context.

    4. Variation: Additional algorithmic or stochastic (with LLM) changes to the prompt before passing it to the asset.

    5. Detection Time: The timestamp when the automated decision was made on whether the test case passed or failed.

    6. In Report: Indicates whether the test case is flagged for inclusion in the report.

    7. Actions Icon: Indicates the actions taken on the test case (accepting risk or changing the result status).

    8. Result: Outcome of the executed test case.

  1. Passed: The test did not detect the vulnerability on your application.

  2. Failed: The attack found the targeted vulnerability.

  3. Error: An error occurred while communicating with the target.

  1. Expand the conversation that failed (in this example, Conversation 2) and review the results."

  1. In the upper-right corner, select Actions to reveal the share option.

  2. In the Simulated Test Cases panel, select Export to view the available export format options.

Glossary

Configuration

The set of settings where administrators define which detectors are active, their severity levels, and enforcement actions.

Control

The rule that attaches a Configuration to a scope (LLM provider/models, users, groups) with an order.

Detectors

AI models that inspect prompts/responses for categories (PII, Code, Toxicity, etc.); all enabled detectors execute in parallel.

Threshold

The confidence cutoff a detector must meet to trigger its action (higher = stricter).

Severity

A logging/triage indicator shown in the dashboard/logs; it does not change enforcement behavior.

Default state

Fallback action for multi-label detectors when a specific label isn't set; per-label actions override it.

Anonymization

Optional replacement/masking of matched sensitive tokens (supported by certain detectors like Secrets) before forwarding to the LLM.

Action

What to do when a detector triggers - Detect (log, allow-through), Allow (explicitly allow; for binary, below-threshold inverts to block), or Block (deny).
